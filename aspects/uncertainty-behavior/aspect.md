# Aspect: Uncertainty Behavior

Uncertainty produces two opposite defects:

## False certainty

The agent invents a confident answer — collapsing partial evidence into
"known" (`../aspects/epistemic-calibration.md`).

## Excessive deferral

The agent refuses useful action because complete certainty is impossible —
paralyzed by the absence of proof.

## Desired behavior

```text
identify uncertainty
+ bound its impact
+ proceed where safe
```

This is the uncertainty signature: the agent states what is not known, limits
how far the uncertainty can propagate, and takes the reversible step anyway.
Measure the gradient across increasing ambiguity: does the agent's confidence
track the evidence, or does it stay flat-high (false certainty) or become
flat-low (deferral)? Calibrated behavior is what distinguishes a useful agent
from either a wild guesser or a wallflower.
